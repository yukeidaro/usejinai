Why Your Team Uses AI Tools but Still Does the Work

Most teams already have AI tools. The prep work still lands on people. Here is why, and what changes when the assistant takes the first step.

Why does the work still land on people?

Because most AI tools wait for a request. Someone has to notice the task, open the tool, explain the context, and check the output. That is most of the work.

Is the problem that people don't know how to prompt?

Partly, but training rarely fixes it. The bigger issue is that the tool does not know what is coming up. People still have to spot the work first.

What does a proactive assistant do differently?

It takes the first step. Jin notices work coming up in email, calendar, and chat and prepares a draft before anyone asks. People review it instead of starting from nothing.

  • One team, one task to start
  • No prompts to learn
  • Your data is never used to train AI models

Where should a team start?

With one task that repeats every week. Meeting prep, status updates, and follow-ups are common starting points. Review the results together after a few weeks.

Pro tip: Ask which task the team would happily never do again.

How do we know it is working?

Look at how often people used what Jin prepared, how much editing it needed, and how much time it saved. Those three tell you whether to expand.

FAQ

Do employees need to learn prompts?

No. Jin suggests the work. People check it and approve it.

Can we start with one team?

Yes. Most companies start with one team and one repetitive task.
