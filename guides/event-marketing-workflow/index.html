The Event Marketing Workflow, End to End

Running an event is more than booking a room. Here is the whole workflow an event marketer runs, stage by stage, and how to turn it into something the rest of the team can repeat.

What are the stages of an event campaign?

Most B2B events follow the same six stages: target, demand gen, venue, speakers, content and follow-up. The order rarely changes. What changes is who runs it and how much of it they remember from last time.

  • Target: the accounts and roles you want in the room.
  • Demand gen: invitations, ads and partner promotion for those accounts.
  • Venue: the shortlist, the booking and the logistics.
  • Speakers: who speaks, what they say and when their slides are due.
  • Content: the landing page, emails, social posts and session copy.
  • Follow-up: personal notes after the event and the handoff to sales.

How do you choose who should be in the room?

Start from the accounts sales wants to move, not from a generic list. Agree on the target accounts and the roles you need from each one before anything else, because every later stage depends on it.

What does demand gen for an event look like?

A short plan for each channel: personal invitations from account owners, a small paid push to the target accounts, and promotion through partners and speakers. Reminders go out on a schedule the team agreed on in advance.

Pro tip: Write down which invitations actually produced registrations. That note is the part of the workflow most teams lose between events.

How do venue and speakers fit in?

Venue and speakers run in parallel with demand gen. The venue needs a shortlist, a booking and a final confirmation. Speakers need an invitation, a brief on the audience and a deadline for their slides.

What content does an event need?

A landing page, an invitation sequence, social posts, session descriptions and a follow-up email. All of it should come from the same brand guidelines and product messaging, so it reads like one campaign.

Why does follow-up decide the result?

The event creates attention. Follow-up turns it into pipeline. Send personal notes while the event is still fresh, and hand the warmest accounts to sales with the context of what they saw and asked.

FAQ

Does this replace the event marketer?

No. The marketer keeps the decisions: who to invite, what to say and which venue to book. Jin prepares and runs the steps in between.

Who sees the captured work?

The marketer sees and edits every workflow first. Managers see workflows and results, not screens.
